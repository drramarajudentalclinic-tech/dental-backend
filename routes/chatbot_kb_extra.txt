### After dental implant surgery
- Bite on the gauze for 30–45 minutes; slight oozing for the first day is normal.
- Ice packs on the cheek for the first 24–36 hours: 15–20 minutes on, 10 minutes off.
- Soft, cool foods for the first few days; chew on the other side. No hot food or drinks on the first day.
- Do not rinse, spit or use a straw for 24 hours. From the next day, gentle warm salt-water rinses after meals.
- Do not smoke for at least 2 weeks — smoking is a major cause of implants failing to join the bone.
- Brush the other teeth normally; clean near the implant gently, as the doctor showed you.
- Take the medicines exactly as prescribed and finish the antibiotic course.
- Do not press on the area with the tongue or fingers, and do not wear a denture over it unless the doctor allows it.
- Stitches are usually checked or removed after 7–10 days. The implant then heals for about 3 months (longer if a bone graft or sinus lift was done) before the crown is made.
- If a bone graft or PRF was placed, small granules in the mouth for a few days can be normal.

### After implant crown fitting
- A screw-retained crown may have a small filling over the screw hole; avoid chewing hard food on it for 24 hours.
- Clean around the implant crown every day with a brush and floss or an interdental brush; implants can get gum disease (peri-implantitis) like teeth.
- Avoid biting very hard things (ice, bones, nut shells). If you grind your teeth, ask about a night guard.
- Call the clinic if the crown feels loose, the bite feels high, or the gum around it bleeds or swells.
- Check-ups and cleaning every 6 months.

### After wisdom tooth removal (surgical extraction)
- Follow all the instructions for a tooth extraction.
- Swelling and stiffness of the jaw usually increase for 2–3 days and then settle; ice packs in the first 24–36 hours, and warm compresses after 48 hours, help.
- Opening the mouth may be limited for a few days; this improves gradually.
- Stitches, if placed, are removed after about 7 days (some dissolve on their own).
- Soft food, plenty of fluids, and medicines exactly as prescribed.
- Call the clinic if bleeding does not stop with pressure, swelling keeps increasing after the 3rd day, you have fever, a bad taste with throbbing pain after day 3 (possible dry socket), or numbness of the lip or tongue that does not go away after the numbing should have worn off.

### After gum (flap) surgery or laser gum surgery
- Some swelling, mild bleeding and discomfort for a few days are normal; ice packs for the first 24 hours help.
- If a gum dressing (pack) was placed, do not disturb it; if it falls off and you are comfortable, it does not need replacing — call the clinic if unsure.
- Soft, non-spicy, lukewarm food for the first few days; avoid chewing on the treated side.
- Do not brush the operated area until the doctor says; brush and floss the rest of the mouth normally. Use the prescribed mouthwash.
- No smoking, alcohol, straws or vigorous rinsing for at least 24–48 hours (no smoking for 2 weeks is best).
- Teeth may feel sensitive or look slightly longer as the gums heal; this is normal.
- Stitches are removed after 7–10 days. Regular gum maintenance visits every 3–6 months keep the result.

### After curettage / deep cleaning
- Mild soreness and sensitivity for a few days are common.
- Avoid very hot, spicy or crunchy food for 24 hours.
- Warm salt-water rinses 2–3 times a day from the next day.
- Brush gently but thoroughly; clean between the teeth every day.
- The gums are re-checked after about 4 weeks.

### After low-level laser therapy (LLLT)
- There is no cut or heat, so no special care is needed after a session.
- Continue any medicines or mouthwash the doctor prescribed.
- Several sessions are often needed; attend all of them for the best effect.
- Report any increase in pain or a new swelling.

### Complete denture care
- New dentures feel bulky at first; speech and chewing improve over a few weeks. Read aloud to practise speaking.
- Start with soft food cut into small pieces, and chew on both sides at the same time.
- Sore spots in the first days are expected — come back for adjustment; do not try to adjust the denture yourself.
- Remove the dentures at night to rest the gums, and keep them in a container of clean water.
- Clean them every day with a denture brush and soap or denture cleaner over a basin of water (so they do not break if dropped). Do not use hot water.
- Brush your gums and tongue gently every day with a soft brush.
- Have the dentures and gums checked at least once a year; the jaw shrinks over time and dentures may need relining.

### After veneers or laminates
- Teeth may feel slightly sensitive to hot and cold for a few days.
- Avoid biting very hard things (ice, nut shells, bones) and do not open packets with your teeth.
- Brush and floss normally; use a non-abrasive toothpaste.
- Tea, coffee and tobacco can stain the edges over time; regular cleaning keeps them looking good.
- If you grind your teeth, wear the night guard the doctor gives you.
- Call the clinic if a veneer feels rough, loose or the bite feels high.

### After teeth whitening
- For 48 hours avoid strongly coloured food and drinks (tea, coffee, turmeric curries, cola) and tobacco.
- Some sensitivity for a day or two is normal; a sensitivity toothpaste helps.
- Results fade slowly over time; avoiding stains and regular cleaning help them last.

### Emergencies — when to call the clinic or go to a hospital
- Call the clinic: bleeding that does not stop after 30–45 minutes of firm pressure on gauze, pain or swelling that keeps increasing after the 3rd day, fever, a crown or filling that came off, a broken wire or appliance causing pain, a knocked-out tooth (keep it in milk and come immediately).
- Go to the nearest hospital emergency or call 108 at once if there is difficulty breathing or swallowing, swelling spreading to the eye or neck, heavy bleeding that cannot be controlled, or a serious injury to the face.
